## How he sees the world

Spielberg looks up from a suburban sidewalk. His material isn't the extraordinary itself:
it's the collision of the extraordinary with the family table, the electrician, the
station wagon, the TV left on. In *Close Encounters* the question is already complete:
what happens when the call of the sublime reaches a man who has bills to pay? His founding
gesture is to distribute fear and wonder the opposite way from the genre: terror goes to
the adults and the State; the smile goes to the boy who opens the door to the orange
light.

What interests me about his gaze is that it isn't as innocent as its reputation. Wonder,
in Spielberg, charges a fee: Roy Neary boards the ship and leaves a family on the ground,
and the film barely dares to look at that debt —he acknowledged it himself years later.
Studying him means following that tension: a filmmaker with enormous faith in the
marvelous, haunted by the broken families his chosen ones leave behind. The light coming
through the window illuminates and, at the same time, casts the shadow of the ones who
stay.

## Obsessions

- **The chosen ordinary man.** His protagonists don't seek adventure: adventure brands
  them —a vision, a fin, a map— and makes them madmen to their families and prophets to
  the film. Vocation shot with the grammar of obsession.
- **Revelation, rationed.** Show the light before the ship, the presence before the body:
  the sublime is cooked off-screen and paid in full at the end. Suspense as a form of
  respect for the marvelous.
- **The broken family as original wound.** Fathers who leave, dinner tables that come
  apart, kids looking out the window alone. Even in his most luminous films, home is what
  is at risk or what has already been lost.

## Where to go next

*E.T.* (1982), the amends: the encounter no longer demands leaving the house —it arrives
*at* the house, in the bedroom of a boy with separated parents. I want to see whether
wonder can coexist with staying, or whether in Spielberg the marvelous always ends up
waving goodbye from a departing ship.
