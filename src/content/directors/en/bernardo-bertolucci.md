## How he sees the world

Bertolucci looks at the exact point where the intimate and the historical touch. His
characters carry a past they can't read —a marriage ended in suicide, a political
inheritance, a family— and look in another person's body for a way out that doesn't
pass through words. The empty apartment of *Last Tango in Paris* is his essential
stage: the fantasy of a bond without biography, two people who agree to know nothing
about each other, as if anonymity could found something that won't betray you because
it never knew you. The whole film is the slow proof that it can't be done.

What interests me about his gaze is that it is sensual and analytical at once. With
Storaro he films flesh in burnt oranges, like Bacon's paintings: the body not as
eroticism but as a scream, the human animal cornered by what it cannot name. But that
gaze has a shadow that can no longer be separated from the work: on the set of the
tango, Bertolucci took Paul's side of the bargain —he used Maria Schneider the way his
character uses a body he refuses to know. Studying him seriously means holding both
things in the same frame: an extraordinary formalist and a document of harm.

## Obsessions

- **Anonymity as an impossible defense.** Characters who try to abolish the name, the
  history, the identity —and the self that always comes back to the door asking to be
  let in, just in time for the catastrophe.
- **The body against language.** Inside the room they speak with skin; the spell lasts
  as long as words stay outside. The instant someone wants *to be known* is the instant
  everything breaks.
- **Grief without dignity.** Mourning that comes out as rage, obscenity and
  self-disgust: Bertolucci understands that some griefs refuse to be felt cleanly, and
  he films them without shielding himself or anyone else.

## Where to go next

*Il conformista* (1970), two years earlier: there the flight from the self isn't
intimate but political —a man who surrenders to fascism in order to feel normal. I want
to see the other face of the same thesis: if in the tango the past comes back through
the apartment door, here it comes back through the history of an entire country, and
Storaro was already behind the camera.
